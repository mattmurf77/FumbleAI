# Owner acquisition and the Home screen

## Product decision

Give someone who plans to stay in their home a reason to use Home Blueprint this week. The first visit should turn something they already have into a useful record, or let them schedule a real job. Subsequent visits should help them act on existing commitments, compare quotes, keep the paperwork and remember what happened.

A homeowner may later choose to share a few facts with an agent. Selling is optional. Home Blueprint can deliver on its promise even if the owner never shares a record.

## The recommended next action

The Home screen has three parts in order: one recommended next action, scheduled to-dos for the selected period, then **What do you want to do today?** navigation. The primary recommendation has an action verb, a short reason and a single destination. Draw candidates from the same household records that drive the other tabs. Offer alternatives without filling the screen with competing recommendation cards. Keep sponsored offers out of task ranking.

| Household state | Example recommendation | Destination and completion condition |
|---|---|---|
| An open task is overdue or due today | **Check the dryer vent** · Due today | Open that exact task. The owner can finish, reschedule or pause it. A completed task leaves the recommendation set. |
| The home is new and the owner has existing records | **Bring in your past work** · Start with a spreadsheet or a permit | Open the import choices. The owner reviews rows or document suggestions before saving. Never require manual entry of their whole property. |
| Work is planned and the owner needs a comparable price | **Get a written estimate** · Bathroom remodel | Open that project’s quote tools. Share an editable scope, then file and compare the returned estimates. |
| A relevant maintenance task is missing | **Clean the gutters** · Seasonal maintenance | Explain applicability, show the scope and date of the US guide range, and let the owner create a to-do or project, request a quote, or hide the idea. |
| A layout would improve organization | **Add your first room** · Organize work by location | Open the existing map builder. The owner can keep logging work without setting up a floor plan. |

Prefer an actual due commitment to suggested work. Re-evaluate after completion, import or other changes. Use a stable ordering for ties and keep dismissal scoped to the household. Do not turn an idea into a scheduled job, loan lead or expense until the owner chooses an action.

## All recommendations, DIY readiness and shopping

The primary card opens its action directly. **All recommendations** is the place to browse the remaining household actions and the catalog of optional jobs. Choose **DIY** or **Hire a pro** in the catalog or on an individual idea. Professional pricing ranges identify the source, date and geography. DIY ranges specify what is priced, such as a replacement filter or a closet kit, and which costs are excluded. A matched full-improvement benchmark can illustrate the range of possible resale contribution. Where the evidence supports maintenance of condition only, the detail says so.

Suggested and manually created to-dos use the same ownership and supply workflow. Match needs against owned equipment and positive, compatible inventory quantities; show the storage location. For partial stock, show how much remains. An unmatched item means the owner should check at home. They can choose a differently named item from their own catalog, confirm the model and location, and reuse its recorded quantity. A homeowner without a floor plan can add, find and edit equipment and supplies under Stuff → Tools & equipment. They explicitly select missing items for the existing shopping list. Saving or reopening a chore must not duplicate requests. Buying an item updates stock and clears the request. A professional handoff links to a named provider or directory and lets the owner prepare a separate written quote request.

## Things to do this period

Home should show a short list of already-scheduled tasks separately from possible additions. Day, week, month and year controls change the date window. Use the owner’s calendar and display meaningful dates. Keep overdue work recognizable and avoid counting a task twice. A recurring chore should appear against its next actionable occurrence rather than dozens of speculative copies. A row opens the real task, and the full To-Dos view retains access to paused and undated work.

## Estimate a job the homeowner describes

A custom to-do or project has the same path to an estimate as a recommendation. Use an entered scope to suggest a starting calculation the owner can inspect. For example, “paint my 12 × 14 bedroom” provides room length and width. Wall height, coats, openings and which surfaces to paint still need a visible assumption or an owner override.

A homeowner can estimate an unfamiliar job by adding materials, work time, tool rentals and fixed fees. Multiply quantities by low and high unit prices; use purchased pack sizes when supplied. Reduce new spending only for compatible, usable stock the owner confirms. Keep the cash cost of DIY separate from hours spent. A required missing price leaves a visible subtotal and a list of what still needs pricing. A saved written quote and a final receipt remain distinct observations. Show the basis, units, currency and date of any supplied guide so the homeowner can replace it with local prices. A custom cost estimate alone supplies no evidence about the home's resale price.

## Bring the records the owner already has

- **Spreadsheets.** Accept Excel or Sheets exports as CSV or tab-separated text. Show column mapping, date precision, currency, excluded rows and duplicates before committing. Keep the original year when a day is unknown. A retry must preserve owner edits.
- **Files and sharing.** Support Files locations enabled on the iPhone, including iCloud Drive and third-party file providers. Accept ownership and improvement documents shared from Mail or Files. A connected file picker grants access to the selected files; a continuous account sync would be a separate integration.
- **Originals and OCR.** Retain original PDFs/images and let the owner confirm any extracted title, date, document number, contractor or amount against the source page. Permit issuance, final inspection, quote amount and an actual payment belong in distinct fields. An owner can file a document without extracting text.
- **Completed work.** Record the task or project, room or whole-property scope, what was serviced/repaired/replaced, completion date or year if known, and optional actual cost. Link supporting documents privately. Let owners reopen the original during maintenance, warranty calls or planning.

## Planning and referrals

The owner compares their project estimate with dated cost guides and written local quotes. An illustrative project-specific resale contribution may use an owner-selected, scope-matched research assumption. Keep its source visible and store it separately from actual spending. A loan payment illustration uses terms entered by the owner. The app does not need to price the entire property or predict buyers to help with these decisions.

A contractor-referral pilot requires verified service areas, appropriate credentials, current availability and a clear commercial disclosure. An improvement-financing pilot requires a product- and jurisdiction-specific compensation model and reviewed lender disclosures. US Regulation X restricts fees for covered settlement-service referrals. Keep the contractor and financing decisions independent. A configured link opens a named provider only after an owner taps it. Sharing the property, documents or contact details requires a separate deliberate handoff. ## Evidence of success

Measure the share of new owners who complete a first useful action, time to first saved task or prior project, repeat use by owners with no selling plans, completion of accepted recommendations, quote comparisons and return to an original document. Track skipped or corrected suggestions, duplicate imports and repeated unwanted recommendations. Measure first-time household acquisition separately from owners who already use another portal. A later partner pilot can measure incremental, deduplicated listings against a comparison group. Returning a user to an already-syndicated listing does not add a home to inventory.

## Release checks

1. Create a home without an address or floor plan. Import a year-only project with an unknown cost and reopen it after relaunch.
2. Confirm a permit reference from OCR. Inspect the original. Verify that its issue date did not become a project completion date.
3. Complete an overdue task. Its recommendation and its time-period row update. Verify recurring and paused cases separately.
4. Accept a source-backed seasonal idea, choose the date and revisit Home. The existing task should supersede its duplicate suggestion.
5. Save two contractor estimates. Compare the original evidence and amounts without changing actual spending. Prepare a quote request without including the address, notes or receipts by default.
6. Preview a Home Brief. Share only selected work and explicitly enabled fields. Restore a purchase on the App Store test environment.
7. Toggle a recommendation between DIY and professional work. Inspect its sourcing and supply list. Match an owned tool by location, select just the missing quantity, save the task twice, and verify one shopping request. Reopen a manually created task and repeat the same checks.
